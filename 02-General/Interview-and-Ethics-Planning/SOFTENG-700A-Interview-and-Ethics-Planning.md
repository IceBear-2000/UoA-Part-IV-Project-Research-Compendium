## Section 1. Ethics requirements

*UoA UAHPEC guidelines, options and recommended path forward*

### 1.1 What does UoA require?

The University of Auckland (UoA) requires ethics approval for virtually all research involving human participants, including research carried out as part of coursework such as this Honours project. This is governed by the University of Auckland Human Participants Ethics Committee (UAHPEC).

> **UoA Policy: Ethics Review of Research Proposals Involving Human Participants (2021).** All research involving human participants carried out by members of the University, including research within teaching sessions and research carried out as part of coursework, must obtain approval from the appropriate ethics committee. Failure to obtain ethics approval when required may constitute research misconduct and may occasion disciplinary action.

Additionally, UAHPEC does not grant retrospective approval, meaning interviews cannot be conducted first and applied for later. Approval must be in place before any data collection begins.

### 1.2 What activities are exempt?

The only meaningful exemption mentioned in UAHPEC documentation is:

> **Exemption: solitary interview with a public figure.** A single, one-off interview with an individual public figure (for example a politician, senior academic or published author) about matters within their public role. This does not extend to a series of interviews, interviews with multiple people, or interviews with members of the general public, students or teachers in a private capacity.

This means the following participant groups we intend to interview do require ethics approval:

- Teachers and facilitators
- Academic coordinators and school administrators
- Students (especially those under 18, which requires additional parental consent layers)
- General public participants
- Entrepreneurs and business people interested in educational ventures

### 1.3 Options available

| Option | Description |
|---|---|
| **Option 1: Full UAHPEC application** | Submit a full ethics application through the UoA Ethics RM system. The supervisor must initiate access for Honours students. Timeline: approximately 6 to 12 weeks depending on submission deadlines. Best for projects that will be formally cited in published research or final reports as primary data. |
| **Option 2: Low-risk / expedited review** | Design the interview study to qualify for expedited (fast-track) low-risk review. Significantly faster (2 to 4 weeks), and the recommended path if interviews proceed. Criteria: no minors, no sensitive topics, anonymised data, voluntary participation, no recording. |
| **Option 3: Avoid ethics entirely** | Restructure requirements elicitation to rely entirely on literature, publicly available data, existing reports and conversations with the supervisor. No human participant interviews are conducted and cited as data. The fastest path, but limits depth of insight. Best if the prototype needs to begin immediately. |

### 1.4 Recommended path forward

**Option 2.** Apply for low-risk / expedited UAHPEC review, designing the interview study carefully to meet low-risk criteria (adult professionals only, no minors, anonymous responses, no audio recording, purely professional topics). This gives real primary data to support design decisions while remaining ethical and academically defensible.

### 1.5 How to qualify for low-risk (expedited) review

| Criteria | How to meet it |
|---|---|
| Participant age | Adults only (18+). Exclude students under 18 entirely from interviews. |
| Topic sensitivity | Keep questions focused on professional workflows, educational delivery challenges and platform needs. Avoid personal, emotional or health-related topics. |
| Anonymity | Do not collect or record names. Refer to participants by role only (for example "Teacher A"). |
| Recording | Do not audio or video record interviews. Use written notes only. |
| Consent | Prepare a simple written consent form confirming voluntary participation and anonymity. |
| Data storage | Store notes securely and delete after project submission. |
| Vulnerable groups | Do not interview anyone in a vulnerable position and avoid people in a dependent relationship with you. |

### 1.6 Immediate next steps

- Confirm we should apply for this approval.
- Get feedback and finalise interview questions; complete a Participant Information Sheet (PIS).
- Get approval and submit the application.
- Do not conduct any formal interviews until written approval is received.
- Since approval will take time, look into literature about what problems most people face in education today, problems in education, and problems in starting an educational enterprise.

### 2.2 Research vocabulary

Definitions written from the perspective of the platform being built, for consistent terminology in interview documents, ethics applications and the final reports.

| Term | Definition (platform-specific) |
|---|---|
| Facilitator | A person who delivers lessons to students using the platform. A facilitator does not need to be a subject-matter expert; they follow the structured lesson flow provided by the platform to guide students through learning activities. |
| Administrator | A person (or organisation) with elevated access who creates, organises and manages lesson content, subject areas, class schedules and facilitator accounts. Administrators are typically the educational experts or coordinators who design the learning experience others deliver. |
| Lesson | A single structured learning session designed by an administrator and delivered by a facilitator, made up of an ordered sequence of steps (for example video, workbook, activity, worksheet). |
| Lesson flow | The step-by-step sequence of activities within a lesson. The core guidance mechanism of the platform: it tells the facilitator exactly what to do at each stage, in what order, reducing reliance on subject expertise. |
| Lesson step / learning block | A single component within a lesson flow: video (embedded instructional video), workbook (page reference and preview), activity (classroom activity instructions) or worksheet (printable task sheet, which may be AI-generated). |
| Facilitator dashboard | The primary interface used by facilitators when delivering a lesson: the current lesson schedule, the step-by-step lesson flow and lesson completion tracking, designed to be intuitive for non-expert educators. |
| Admin panel | The interface used by administrators to create lesson content, organise class schedules, manage facilitator accounts, upload videos, build lesson flows and review AI-generated worksheets before publication. |

## Section 3. Interview methodology and question bank

### 3.1 Interview methodology: semi-structured interviews

This approach is appropriate because:

- It provides a consistent base of questions across all interviewees, enabling comparison of responses.
- It allows the interviewer to follow up on unexpected but valuable insights.
- It is well suited to a 15-minute session, balancing depth with efficiency.
- It is recognised as low-risk by UAHPEC, especially when questions are non-sensitive and participants are adults in professional roles.

### 3.2 Interview structure (15-minute format)

| Time | Phase and purpose |
|---|---|
| 0:00 to 1:00 | **Introduction and consent.** Briefly introduce yourself and the project. Confirm the participant has read and signed the consent form. Remind them participation is voluntary and responses will be anonymous. |
| 1:00 to 2:00 | **Warm-up question.** A single open question about their background and role, to put them at ease and establish context. |
| 2:00 to 11:00 | **Core questions (role-specific).** Four to six structured questions tailored to the participant's role, with follow-up probes. The primary data-gathering phase. |
| 11:00 to 14:00 | **Platform concept validation.** Briefly describe the platform concept (two to three sentences) and ask one or two reaction or validation questions. |
| 14:00 to 15:00 | **Close.** Ask if anything important has not been covered. Thank them. Confirm data will be anonymised and used only for this project. |

### 3.3 Facilitation guidelines

- Listen actively; do not interrupt or lead the participant toward a particular answer.
- Use probes such as "Can you tell me more about that?", "What does that look like in practice?", "Why is that a challenge?"
- Take written notes only; do not record audio or video (to remain within low-risk ethics criteria).
- If a participant strays off-topic, gently redirect: "That's really useful. Coming back to..."
- Never share what other participants have said.
- If a participant seems uncomfortable with a question, skip it; participation is voluntary.

### 3.4 Platform description (for validation questions)

> "We are building a facilitator-based education platform. The idea is that educational experts design lessons centrally, and those lessons are then delivered step by step by facilitators, people who may not be subject experts, through a guided digital interface. The platform also includes an AI feature that can generate printable worksheets from a topic prompt. Think of it as a structured guide that lets almost anyone deliver a high-quality lesson."

### 3.5 Question bank

**I. General questions (all interviewees)**

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| G1 | Can you briefly describe your current role and your connection to education? |
| G2 | In your experience, what is the biggest challenge in delivering consistent, quality education, especially in areas where resources or qualified teachers are limited? |
| G3 | Have you encountered situations where a lesson or topic had to be delivered by someone who wasn't fully confident in the subject? What happened? |
| G4 | How do you currently access or manage educational content and lesson materials? |
| G5 | What would make the biggest difference to the quality of education being delivered in your context? |

**II. Academic coordinator / school principal**

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| C1 | How do you currently assign teachers or facilitators to classes? What happens when a qualified teacher is unavailable? |
| C2 | How much of your time is spent dealing with inconsistencies in how lessons are being delivered across different classrooms? |
| C3 | If you had a platform that guided facilitators step by step through a lesson, how would that change your day-to-day operations? |
| C4 | What information would you need to see as an administrator: class completion, facilitator progress, student engagement? |
| C5 | What concerns would you have about your staff using a platform like this? |

**III. Classroom teacher (experienced)**

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| T1 | Walk me through how you typically prepare and deliver a lesson from start to finish. |
| T2 | What parts of lesson delivery do you think require genuine subject expertise, and what parts could be done by a well-guided non-expert? |
| T3 | Have you ever used any digital tools to support lesson delivery? What worked and what didn't? |
| T4 | If you were designing lessons to be delivered by a non-expert, what would you absolutely need to include? |
| T5 | How do you adapt when students are at very different ability levels in the same classroom? |

**IV. Non-expert facilitator / teaching assistant**

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| F1 | What is your background, and how did you end up delivering lessons to students? |
| F2 | What is the hardest part of facilitating a lesson in a subject you're not fully confident in? |
| F3 | What kind of guidance or support would make you feel more confident when delivering a lesson? |
| F4 | If you had a screen in front of you telling you exactly what to do next, what would you need it to show you? |
| F5 | What would make you trust a platform enough to use it in a real classroom? |

**V. Person wanting to open a school / education venture**

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| E1 | What has stopped you or made it difficult to set up your own educational programme or school? |
| E2 | What would you need in place before you felt confident enough to start delivering classes? |
| E3 | How important is it to you that the people delivering lessons are subject experts rather than well-guided non-experts? |
| E4 | What would a platform need to offer for you to trust it as the backbone of your educational operation? |
| E5 | From a business perspective, what pricing or access model would make this viable for you? |

**VI. Homeschooling parent**

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| H1 | How do you currently structure and deliver lessons to your child at home? |
| H2 | Which subjects do you feel least confident teaching, and how do you handle those? |
| H3 | What resources or tools do you currently rely on most heavily, and why? |
| H4 | How do you track whether your child is progressing and engaging with the material? |
| H5 | What would you want from a digital platform that could help you deliver more structured lessons? |

**VII. Adult student (reflecting on past K–12 experience)**

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| S1 | Think back to your schooling: what made a lesson feel well taught rather than poorly taught? |
| S2 | Did you ever have lessons delivered by someone who clearly wasn't a subject expert? How did that affect your learning? |
| S3 | What kinds of activities or materials helped you learn best when you were in school? |
| S4 | How important was it to you that lessons felt structured and organised, rather than improvised? |
| S5 | If you were designing a better classroom experience for students like your younger self, what would you prioritise? |

**VIII. Platform validation questions (all interviewees)**, asked after describing the platform (3.4):

| # | Question |
|---|---|
