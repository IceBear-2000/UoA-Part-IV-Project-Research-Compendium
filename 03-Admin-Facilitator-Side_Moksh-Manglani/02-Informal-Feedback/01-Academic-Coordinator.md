**In one paragraph.** She backed the problem strongly and pushed back on one assumption. Of her 35 teachers she counts five or six who go beyond the basics, and when a teacher leaves, the cover teacher keeps the written work and drops the explanation. But she does not think running the room is the easy part: "Even if you know how to teach, if you don't have classroom management then you cannot teach."

> Quotes are lightly tidied for readability.

## Key points

1. **Good teachers are rare, and it shows in numbers.** Of 35 teachers, she names five or six "who really work for the children". The rest are "coming, teaching and going". From a batch of 10 to 15 trainees she expects one or two strong ones.
2. **Teachers leave, and not only for pay.** She agreed that trained teachers leave for a better package, and added marriage and pregnancy among younger staff.
3. **When a teacher leaves, the explanation is the first thing to go.** The load lands on other teachers, who get the written work done but skip the explaining. She has covered many classes herself.
4. **Small children take it hardest.** In nursery and kindergarten they cry for their own teacher and refuse to sit in another class. The emotional pull is weaker in higher classes.
5. **Nobody is sent into a class cold.** Even an existing teacher switching subject gets one to two hours of briefing and at least a day to prepare, because "when the subject changes, pedagogy, everything will change". A new teacher needs about a week, and only works out if eager to learn.
6. **A good lesson is a timed, ordered plan.** Learning objective first, then material prepared in advance, the method, delivery inside a 40-minute period with time for students to talk, a short check in the last one or two minutes, and a stated learning outcome, all written in a lesson plan.
7. **Teachers should speak less.** "Teachers have to speak less and we have to give more space to the children."
8. **She likes the idea, with one condition: videos cannot be one-way.** Teaching videos she has seen are boring; a viewer watches once, decides they know it and switches off. Pausing the video and asking questions "impacts more".
9. **Classroom management comes before teaching.** Her main pushback. A new person needs the chapter, the teaching material, instructions for the activity and "classroom tricks".
10. **The right facilitator is a patient listener.** Loving, caring, some humour, energetic, active, and above all patient enough to listen to students. She also pointed to the four Cs: communication, collaboration, creativity and critical thinking.
11. **Children differ, and one test for everyone hides it.** "A teacher should know what is special in each and every child." She wants assessment in different forms for different children, which is the opening for Part 2 of the project.
12. **Parents can sink a good method.** The school dropped a set of activity-based books she rated highly because parents would not accept the open-ended questions.

## Where she disagrees with the model

- **"The facilitator just clicks through" is too light.** She expects a new person to be taught how to hold a class first. This became a limitation and a future-work item: add classroom-management guidance to the walkthrough.
- **She would ask the facilitator how they want to teach it.** Her instinct with a new person is to hear their ideas first. StudyBuddy fixes the flow. This tension is named in the report's Discussion.
- **She would not put a stranger in front of nursery or kindergarten.** This supports stating an age range, or keeping the same facilitator with a class.
