## Answers

**What happens when a good teacher leaves? What does it cost, and how long before the class is back to normal?**
It is a big problem. It affects the school's reputation, some students leave with the teacher, and it motivates other teachers to leave as well.

**When you are short, how do you decide who takes the class?**
Teachers give a month's notice, which gives time to recruit. When a teacher leaves abruptly or has to be let go, academic coordinators in the schools take the teaching while a new teacher is recruited. It takes time for students to settle in, because "students tend to subconsciously compare teachers".

**Between your strongest and weakest teacher in the same subject and year, how different is what students get?**
A good teacher teaches from the heart: they care, put in effort and make sure students understand. A weak teacher just passes time and does not care about the students.

**What does it cost to bring a new teacher to the point where you trust them alone in a room?**
There is a three-day training period, which is often insufficient and is being extended to a week. The resources it takes to make a teacher good vary widely, but every case needs months of polishing and practice before the teacher is good and comfortable with students.

**If someone without a teaching qualification could deliver a lesson your best teacher designed, what would have to be true before you allowed it?**
Good training is always required. A buddy system helps a lot. A code of conduct would be needed so that everyone recruited knows the organisation's rules and regulations.

**What does a well-delivered lesson look like, and how do you tell from outside the room?** Not answered; referred to a colleague.
