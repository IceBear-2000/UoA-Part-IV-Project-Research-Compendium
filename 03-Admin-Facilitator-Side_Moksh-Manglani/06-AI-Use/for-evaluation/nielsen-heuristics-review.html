Nielsen Heuristics Review
Usability review

StudyBuddy against Nielsen's 10 heuristics

Nielsen's heuristics are ten general rules of thumb for good interface design, from Jakob Nielsen (Nielsen Norman Group). Below, each one in plain language, what StudyBuddy actually does about it, and why that specific choice is a good fit for the rule.

This covers the whole app — admin panel and the facilitator-facing side that runs an actual lesson.

Visibility of system status

The app should always show what's happening, quickly.

What we did

Running a lesson, the step list on the left shows every block as done (✓), active, or locked (🔒), plus a live "X of Y complete" badge. Both update the moment a facilitator marks a step done.

Why it works

A facilitator mid-lesson never has to guess what's finished or what's next — the state of the whole lesson is visible at a glance, not hidden behind a click.

Match between the system and the real world

Use words and pictures people already recognise, not internal jargon.

What we did

Class cards were rebuilt to look like Canvas course cards — a banner image on top, name and count below — instead of a plain list.

Why it works

Most facilitators have used an LMS like Canvas before. Borrowing that exact pattern means they already know what a class card is and how to use it, with zero learning.

User control and freedom

People make mistakes. Give them an easy way out.

What we did

Deleting a lesson block takes two clicks — the button flips to "Click again to confirm?" for 3 seconds, then quietly reverts if you don't.

Why it works

It stops an accidental single click from destroying content, without a disruptive pop-up dialog you have to read and dismiss every time.

Consistency and standards

The same thing should always look and behave the same way.

What we did

All three admin themes (Warm, Studio, Ledger) are built from one shared set of components and one token system — colour, spacing, radius. Only the tokens change per theme.

Why it works

A button behaves like a button and a card behaves like a card everywhere in the app, no matter which theme is active — nothing to relearn page to page.

Error prevention

Stop the mistake before it happens, not just after.

What we did

Changing your account password requires typing the current password first — it's checked against Supabase before the new one is accepted.

Why it works

It blocks the one genuinely risky scenario — someone at an unlocked, signed-in laptop silently changing another admin's password — before it can happen.

Recognition rather than recall

Show information, don't make people remember it.

What we did

Every nested page — admin and facilitator side — shows a breadcrumb: Classes › Grade 1 › Maths. Running a lesson shows the same trail up to the lesson name.

Why it works

Nobody has to remember which grade or subject they came from to get back — it's written on the page the whole time.

Flexibility and efficiency of use

Let beginners do it the plain way, and give experts a faster way too.

What we did

"Create lesson" from inside a subject page pre-fills that class and subject. The same button from the main Lessons page opens a blank form instead.

Why it works

An admin who's already drilled into "Grade 1 › Maths" skips two dropdowns they'd otherwise fill in by hand — without changing anything for someone starting from scratch.

Aesthetic and minimalist design

Only show what's actually needed. Extra stuff competes for attention.

What we did

The Overview dashboard was rebuilt around five focused stat tiles, three quick links, and a recent-activity list — replacing an earlier version that felt cluttered.

Why it works

The first screen an admin sees now answers "what needs my attention" in one glance, instead of competing bits of information with no clear order.

Help users recognise, diagnose, and recover from errors

When something goes wrong, say so in plain words — and say what to do next.

What we did

If the AI worksheet tool can't read a video or file, it doesn't fail silently or throw a stack trace — it lists it in plain language: "Couldn't get a transcript for 'Warm-up song' — no captions available."

Why it works

The admin knows exactly what failed and why, in words they understand, and can still finish the task — the rest of the worksheet still gets generated.

Help and documentation

Ideally nobody needs it — but make it easy to find when they do.

What we did

The settings menu has a "Project docs" section linking straight to the design-exploration record and the worksheet-generator spec.

Why it works

Anyone new to the project — a teammate, a grader — can see the reasoning behind decisions from inside the app itself, without it cluttering the main screens for everyone else.
