# 07 · Ethics

The story of the human ethics application for Part 1, why it was not used in the end, and what was learned. The application documents themselves are not included, because no participant was ever recruited under them.

## What was planned

In May 2026 the plan for requirements elicitation was a formal interview study: about ten semi-structured interviews of around 15 minutes with academic coordinators, teachers, facilitators and students, using a fixed question bank across seven participant profiles and analysed by thematic analysis. The supervisor's advice at the time was to make it systematic (the same questions for everyone), scientific and repeatable, and to allow about three months for ethics approval.

## The application

| Stage | What happened |
|---|---|
| May to June | Application prepared for the University of Auckland Human Participants Ethics Committee (UAHPEC), using the University's templates: a participant information sheet, a consent form, an interview protocol, and an information sheet and consent form for organisations |
| Two pre-screen rounds | Changes made before full review: the supervisor became the principal investigator; recruitment moved from direct approaches to indirect recruitment through intermediaries; AI tools for any sensitive data were limited to those available through institutional login; a cross-border (India to New Zealand) data transfer was disclosed; data storage moved to the University Research Drive; and the claim of anonymity was corrected to confidentiality |
| 27 August | **Conditional approval** (reference UAHPEC32076), with six conditions to meet before any research could begin. They covered a form error about the start date, organisational permission and the full list of recruitment networks, aligning the data withdrawal timeline across documents, narrowing the answer on commercialisation, an optional suggestion to consult the Faculty's Māori ethics advisor, and adding Head of School contact details to the information sheets |
| 15 September | **Pivot.** With the conditions still open, recruitment and organisational sign-off still to come, and the final report due on 18 October, there was not enough time to run and analyse ten interviews properly. On the supervisor's advice, the formal study was replaced with informal feedback chats, written up as design input rather than research data. No approval is needed for this, and no participant was recruited under the application |

The file remained open after the pivot; the University's six-month window for meeting the conditions ran to 27 February 2027.

## What replaced it

Informal feedback from practitioners and reviewers, chosen for relevant experience, with notes linked to the design decisions they supported, changed or challenged. See [`02-Informal-Feedback`](../02-Informal-Feedback/). The report is careful to describe this as feedback, never as interviews or research findings.

## Reflection

- **Ethics is part of the project plan, not a step before it.** Three months was the supervisor's estimate in May, and it proved accurate. A study that depends on approval needs to start the application at the very beginning of the year, or be designed so the project does not depend on it.
- **The process improved the research design even though the study did not run.** Indirect recruitment, confidentiality rather than anonymity, institutional AI tools for sensitive data and a clear withdrawal timeline are all better practice, and they shaped how the informal feedback was handled (by role, with consent to be referenced).
- **Pivoting was the right call.** A rushed study of ten interviews in four weeks, alongside a 12,000-word report, would have produced weaker evidence than honest, clearly labelled design feedback combined with a technical evaluation.

## For a future student

If you want to run the classroom study this project recommends (see [`05-Future-Work-and-Handover`](../../05-Future-Work-and-Handover/)), you will need full UAHPEC approval before contacting anyone. Start early, involve your supervisor as principal investigator, decide every recruitment network in advance (each organisation needs its own signed permission), and keep the withdrawal and data-retention wording identical across every document.
