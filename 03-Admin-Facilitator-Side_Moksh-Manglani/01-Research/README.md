# 01 · Research

The research behind Part 1: the six criteria used to compare models of education, the gap they reveal, the evidence that a facilitator-based model could fill it, and the benchmark of StudyBuddy against existing platforms. The full April literature review is in [`01-Submissions/02-Literature-Review/Moksh-Manglani`](../../01-Submissions/02-Literature-Review/); this page is the condensed, reusable version.

## The six criteria

Defined in the literature review and used unchanged in Chapters 3 and 6 of the final report, so the platform is judged on the same scale as the models it is compared with.

| Criterion | Definition | Key sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Personalisation** | The ability to respond to differences in learner ability, pace and need | Hooshyar et al. (2024) |
| **Decoupled delivery** | How far educational expertise can be separated from direct classroom instruction | UNESCO (2024); Veine et al. (2022) |
| **Structured guidance** | Clear instructional sequences, step-by-step lesson flows and defined support materials that reduce ambiguity in delivery | Piper et al. (2018); Snilstveit et al. (2016); World Bank (2018) |
| **Accessibility** | The ability to overcome geographic, economic and institutional barriers to quality education | UNESCO (2020); World Bank (2025) |
| **Adaptability** | How far the model can be adjusted across learners, subjects, curricula and contexts | Achuthan (2025); Huang et al. (2026) |
| **Engagement** | How well the model sustains learner attention, participation and involvement | Garrison et al. (1999); Jordan (2015); Means et al. (2010) |

## The gap

Existing models split into two groups: those that deliver **quality** through expert-led, resource-intensive teaching, and those that deliver **access** through low-cost content. What is missing is a model that combines accessibility, structured guidance, engagement and decoupled delivery, and in particular a platform designed to guide a non-expert facilitator through live delivery of a lesson an expert designed.

## Evidence that the gap can be filled

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Structured pedagogy programmes produced substantial literacy gains in Kenya | Piper et al. (2018) |
| Structured pedagogy is one of the most effective intervention types in low- and middle-income countries | Snilstveit et al. (2016) |
| Structured lesson plans and teacher support are high-impact for foundational learning | World Bank (2018) |
| Learning assistants contribute meaningfully as facilitators when trained and given structured responsibilities | Veine et al. (2022) |
| Alternative learning system facilitators have meaningful impact despite limited formal training, with support in place | Daga-As and Guntalidad (2025) |
| Non-digital precedents for the same principle | Pratham (Teaching at the Right Level), BRAC |

The same studies attach a condition, and it shaped the design: facilitators succeed when they are **trained, given defined responsibilities and supported**, and a structured programme helps only when it is **delivered as designed**. StudyBuddy answers this with approval before access (screening), the locked walkthrough (induction) and the chat to admins (support).

## Benchmark against existing platforms (Report Table 4)

Platforms built around the same core concept were grouped. Ratings are Strong, Partial or Limited, based on public descriptions and hands-on use.

| Kind of platform | Personalisation | Decoupled delivery | Structured guidance | Accessibility | Adaptability | Engagement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Learning management systems (Moodle, Google Classroom) | Limited | Limited | Limited | Partial | Strong | Partial |
| Self-paced content (Khan Academy, Kolibri) | Partial | Partial | Limited | Strong | Partial | Limited |
| Programme-based (LEAD School, Bridge, Pratham) | Limited | Strong | Strong | Partial | Limited | Partial |
| **StudyBuddy** | Limited | **Strong\*** | **Strong** | Partial | **Strong** | Partial\* |

\*Rated from the design, not from classroom use. Data: [`table-4.csv`](../00-Report-Figures-and-Tables/tables/table-4.csv).

StudyBuddy is the only row combining strong decoupled delivery, strong structured guidance and strong adaptability. It falls short on accessibility (it needs a device and an internet connection, where Kolibri works offline), on personalisation (every student sees the same lesson) and on engagement (which depends on the lesson and the facilitator). The personalisation and engagement gaps are what Part 2, May's learner insights, addresses.

## Limits of this research

The ratings are qualitative judgements, not measurements. The benchmark rests on published descriptions and hands-on use, not on classroom trials. None of these methods measures learning outcomes.
